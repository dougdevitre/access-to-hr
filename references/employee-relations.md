# Employee Relations, Investigations & ADA — Workday County HR Reference

## Overview

Employee relations covers complaints, workplace investigations, accommodation requests and
the day-to-day problems between employees and supervisors. In county government each of these
can become a public record, a civil service appeal, a grievance or a charge of discrimination.
The goal is the same every time: a prompt, fair, documented process.

> ⚠️ LEGAL NOTE: Educational information only, not legal advice. Investigations and
  accommodation decisions carry legal risk. Check with your state labor agency or an
  employment attorney — for a county, usually the county counselor — before deciding a
  discrimination, harassment or retaliation complaint, before denying an accommodation, and
  before discipline that follows a complaint.

---

## Intake: The First 24 Hours

When a complaint arrives (in person, email, hotline, through a supervisor):

1. **Thank the person; do not promise confidentiality.** Promise discretion and need-to-know sharing.
2. **Assess safety.** Threats, violence or immediate danger → law enforcement / security first. In danger now: 911.
3. **Classify the issue:**
   - Discrimination, harassment or retaliation based on a protected characteristic
   - Accommodation request (disability, pregnancy, religion)
   - Policy violation, misconduct, workplace conflict
   - Wage-and-hour complaint
   - Safety or whistleblower concern
4. **Interim measures** if needed (schedule change, reporting-line change, administrative leave). Do not move the complainant to a worse position — that can look like retaliation.
5. **Notify** HR Director; notify county counselor for anything involving protected characteristics, elected officials, department heads, law enforcement, or possible criminal conduct.
6. **Open a case file** outside general HR folders (restricted access).

## Retaliation — The Risk That Outlasts the Complaint

Per EEOC retaliation guidance:
- **Participation** in the EEO process (filing a complaint, serving as a witness) is protected.
- **Opposition** to a practice the employee reasonably believes violates EEO law is protected.
- A materially adverse action is one that "might deter a reasonable person from engaging in protected activity."

**Practice:**
- Tell the complainant and witnesses that retaliation is prohibited and how to report it
- Brief the supervisor in writing
- Review any later discipline, schedule change or evaluation of the complainant with HR before it happens
- Retaliation claims can succeed even when the underlying complaint does not

## Workplace Investigations

### Choose the Investigator

| Situation | Investigator |
|---|---|
| Routine policy violation, low risk | Trained HR partner |
| Harassment or discrimination allegation | Senior HR or outside investigator |
| Allegation against department head, elected official or HR | Outside investigator through county counsel |
| Possible crime | Pause; counsel and law enforcement decide sequence |
| Law enforcement officer conduct | Internal affairs process; see references/public-safety-hr.md |

### Investigation Steps

1. **Plan:** allegations, policy at issue, witnesses, documents, timeline
2. **Interview complainant:** what, when, where, who saw, effect, what outcome they want
3. **Collect documents:** emails, texts on county devices, Workday records, schedules
4. **Interview witnesses:** open questions; one at a time; no sharing of others' statements
5. **Interview respondent:** give the allegations; let them respond fully
6. **Weingarten:** union-represented employees may request a union representative at an investigatory interview that could lead to discipline — see references/labor-relations.md
7. **Garrity (public employers):** in *Garrity v. New Jersey*, 385 U.S. 493 (1967), the Supreme Court held that the Constitution "prohibits use in subsequent criminal proceedings of statements obtained under threat of removal from office." When an employee may face criminal exposure, get counsel's guidance before compelling a statement.
8. **Weigh the evidence:** credibility factors (consistency, corroboration, motive, plausibility)
9. **Findings:** substantiated, not substantiated, or inconclusive — for each allegation
10. **Report:** facts, findings, policy analysis; recommendations go to the decision maker, not into the findings
11. **Close the loop:** tell complainant and respondent the investigation is complete and, in general terms, whether action was taken

### Documentation

- Interview notes: date, time, people present, questions and answers
- Store in the Confidential — Investigation category (see references/records-management.md)
- **Missouri:** investigation records relating to employees may be closable under the Sunshine
  Law (RSMo 610.021(13)); counsel decides closure on each request

## ADA Reasonable Accommodation

Per EEOC enforcement guidance:
- An employee may use "plain English" and need not mention the ADA or use the phrase
  "reasonable accommodation" — any request for a change at work for a medical reason starts the process
- The request is "the first step in an informal, interactive process between the individual and the employer"
- Undue hardship means "significant difficulty or expense," judged against the employer's
  resources and circumstances

### Interactive Process Steps

1. Acknowledge the request in writing within a few business days
2. Ask what limitation affects the job and what the employee suggests
3. If the disability or need is not obvious, request reasonable medical documentation (limited to the need)
4. Identify the essential functions of the job (use the job description; see references/job-classification.md)
5. Explore options: equipment, schedule, telework, reassignment to a vacant position, leave
6. Decide and document: option chosen, alternatives considered, reasons
7. Follow up after 30–60 days to confirm it works

**Watch out:**
- Supervisors often receive the request first. Train them to forward it to HR the same day.
- Keep medical information in the Confidential — Medical category, separate from the personnel file.
- Leave after FMLA is exhausted can still be a reasonable accommodation.

### Pregnancy and Religion

- **PWFA:** accommodation for known limitations related to pregnancy, childbirth or related
  medical conditions; employers with 15+ employees including state and local governments;
  may not require leave if another accommodation works (EEOC)
- **Religious accommodation:** schedule swaps, dress and grooming exceptions, prayer breaks —
  under Title VII, *Groff v. DeJoy* (No. 22-174, decided June 29, 2023) held that an employer
  must show the burden would result in "substantial increased costs in relation to the conduct
  of its particular business"; "more than a de minimis cost" is not enough
  `[VERIFY: U.S. Reports citation — commonly cited as 600 U.S. 447]`
